# Virtual ATC — roadmap after v1 core (2026-10-25)

This document replaces the "Approach/Center, IFR routes, SID/STAR" item of §3 (non-goals) of
`2026-10-19-virtual-atc-design.md`. Everything else in that spec still stands. Each project below
gets its own design document, plan, and review, in this order (user decision, 2026-10-25).

Done so far: plan 1 (text core, Ground + Tower circuit at EPKK), plan 2 (voice, X-Plane 11
client/plugin, squawk, follow-me), plan 3a (Polish). All merged into `main`.

1. **Procedures and all positions** — `2026-10-25-procedures-and-positions-design.md`.
   SID/STAR/approaches from the X-Plane navdata, VFR points from AIP Poland, and the missing
   positions: Kraków Delivery, Kraków Approach, and the adjacent units a flight is handed to.
   Tested by the user on a Cessna 172 G1000: IFR departures and arrivals, VFR entry and exit.
1a. **Runway in use: stability, pilot request, amended clearance** (added 2026-10-28 after the
   first in-sim IFR departure: the cleared SID and the runway taxied to differed after calm
   wind). (a) The runway in use stays until its tailwind exceeds 5 kt (PANS-ATM ch. 7.2); calm or
   light variable wind never switches it. (b) The pilot may request a departure runway ("request
   departure runway zero seven"): granted with that runway's SID when its tailwind is at most
   5 kt, else "unable, runway in use two five"; Ground and Tower then use that runway for the
   flight. (c) A runway change after the clearance is never silent: Ground or Tower says
   "runway changed, runway zero seven, new departure SUDOL one Foxtrot" and requires the
   readback before taxi or take-off. Also from that flight: Delivery treats a request naming
   neither IFR nor VFR as VFR silently; ask "say flight rules" instead.
   More from the same session: (d) a call addressed to another station on a frequency
   ("Krakow Approach, …" on Tower) gets "you are on Kraków Tower, contact Kraków Approach one
   two one decimal zero seven five", not "say again"; (e) a server restart silently drops every
   clearance: keep the strips across restarts, or have the client tell the pilot to start
   again; (f) Approach answers a departure report from an aircraft without a flight plan with
   "no flight plan, say intentions" and can issue an IFR clearance in the air.
   **Done (plan 5, 2026-10-29), on branch `insim-fixes`:**
   - (a) runway in use kept up to 5 kt tailwind — de1cbaa; "say flight rules" — f8da097.
   - (b) pilot's departure runway request — ef47ffc, 743c339.
   - (c) runway change after the clearance announced and read back — 70a5a53, b5142eb.
   - (d) call to another station answered with "this is …, contact …" — 09b519e.
   - (e) strips kept across server restarts — 45b5253.
   - (f) "no flight plan, say intentions" and the IFR clearance in the air — 90f360c.
     The airborne clearance has no SID or route: cleared to the destination, climb to the
     cruise level, squawk.
1b. **Second airport: Katowice (EPKT).** Full ATIS, Delivery, Ground, Tower and Approach at EPKT
   from the same sources (X-Plane apt.dat and CIFP `EPKT.dat`, AIP Poland AD 2 EPKT), replacing the
   Katowice boundary stub of project 1. Two goals: a complete stand-to-stand scenario
   EPKK→EPKT and back, and proof that a new airport is added with data only. Any code change it
   needs is a project-1 shortcoming and is fixed in the shared code. This lifts the v1
   "EPKK only" non-goal for EPKT.
   Replaced on 2026-10-29 by the airport wizard (`2026-10-29-airport-wizard-design.md`):
   phase 1 (wizard and data) is merged; EPKT becomes its phase 3.
2. **Traffic from a real schedule.** A one-time export of about two days (one weekday, one weekend
   day) of EPKK arrivals and departures from OpenSky, stored as a data file; no network access at
   run time. The traffic follows the X-Plane clock (a simulated weekday plays the exported
   weekday). The server flies the bots with a simple flight model along the procedures from
   project 1; the plugin draws them in X-Plane's AI/multiplayer slots with stock X-Plane 11 models
   (B738, MD-80, SF-50, Baron, King Air, C172, Columbia 400). No heavy aircraft (EPKK runway).
   Bots read back correctly; no invented mistakes. Runway and weather come from X-Plane.
   Check the OpenSky terms before the export is committed to a public repository.
3. **Fictional general-aviation operator.** OpenSky shows almost no GA at EPKK, so a fictional
   operator (SP-xxx registrations) flies to the 11 airfields within 50 NM (EPKP, EPZR, EPKM, EPKW,
   EPBA, EPKT, EPNL, EPNT, EPPC, EPGL, EPRG): departures every 10–15 min in the quietest daylight
   hours, each aircraft returns after 1–2 h for a full stop, and is removed 5 min after its engine
   is shut down on the apron.
4. **Scenario 3: immediate landing (military activity).** "All stations, stop transmitting,
   distress traffic"; strict priority (distress and fuel first, then the aircraft closest to final,
   then a tight conveyor at minimum spacing); short directive phrases, vectors, early landing
   clearances; diversion of the rest to alternates, fanned out and handed to adjacent units.
5. **Helicopters.** The user will fly helicopters too. Own design and plan; helicopter flights in
   the export are skipped until then.
6. **The rest of the original spec.** Instructor (scenario 1), radar approach SRA/PAR (scenario 2,
   on top of Approach from project 1), controller character and pilot memory, Ollama decider and
   decision journal.
7. **More simulators** (added 2026-10-29). Lifts the v1 non-goal "MSFS, X-Plane 12". The server
   (speech, engine, stations) stays simulator-independent; each simulator needs four things:
   telemetry to the client, the airport layout (runways, taxiways, stands), SID/STAR/approaches,
   and the in-sim text window. Own design and plan per simulator, in this order:
   - (a) **X-Plane 12.** Closest to what exists: the same UDP RREF telemetry, apt.dat and CIFP
     formats and plugin SDK. Mostly verifying the dataref names, the apt.dat 1200 additions and
     the install paths (`xplane_root`, Custom Data navdata), then testing in the sim.
   - (b) **Microsoft Flight Simulator 2020** and (c) **2024.** Telemetry through SimConnect
     (Windows only: the client runs next to the simulator, the server may stay on Linux, they
     already talk over WebSocket). The hard part is data: MSFS keeps airport layouts and navdata
     in binary BGL scenery files, so the layout and the procedures need another source (a BGL
     reader, a Little Navmap database export, or a navdata provider such as Navigraph, which is
     paid). The text window becomes an in-sim toolbar panel or a separate window. 2024 is
     checked separately: its scenery and SimConnect changes may differ from 2020.
